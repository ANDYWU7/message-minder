- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: I can't miss December 18, 2026, 5:00 pm Eastern to get my teacher to register me, and I can't miss December 23, 2026, at 5:00pm Eastern/2:00pm Pacific, when I have to submit it.
## The problem
- The young and elderly people struggle with recognizing things like impersonation or suspicious links, because they don't now much about basic internet safety practices and being able to identify these scams.
- Impact area: Safety and kindness
- Why it matters (impact): If this project works, it will help prevent a lot of financial losses because of scams. It will also help prevent things like stolen accounts, compromised personal information, or fear of using online services.
- Evidence it’s real: According to the FBI, American adults aged 60 and older reported losing over $7.7 billion.
https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com
- What people told me: I made a form, and for one of the responses, someone stated that they got scammed and they would like a tool to help identify them. In fact, a lot of the responses stated that they have gotten scammed at least once before.
## My solution
- My AI’s job: My AI helps by identifying possible scams and informing the user on what to do next, which works better than judgement, because the users themselves are the ones who can't judge whether something is a scam or not.
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Google messages has real-time scam protection that can identify different types of scams. Even though it is real-time, it has to rely on the app itself and can't recognize scams outside of messaging, like social media. FTC Pass It On has information. It is not live, and users will have to read from the website to figure out about scams themselves. My App could both explain what the scam is and how to respond. It's also straightforward and not confusing to use.
- Safety plan: - The AI could say a real scam is safe, or give bad advice after someone has shared a password.
- A harsh answer could shame or frighten someone who has already been scammed.
- People may accidentally paste names, phone numbers, addresses, passwords, account details, or private messages.
## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. The user can input a message or link to check for scams.
2. The user can receive a detailed explanation of why something might be a scam.
3. The user can get step-by-step advice on what to do if scammed.
4. The user can report suspicious messages or links for further analysis.
5. The user can learn about common signs of scams through simple tips.
6. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.

## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers: The AI should be able to respond after users submit a possibly suspicious text message, then analyzes for a further explanation.
- Its job: My AI's job is to help identify scams and prevent the user from getting scammed by one.
- Who it talks to and how to sound: My AI is talking to the user, and it should sound clear and concise.
- Facts it must use: Scammers often pressure people to act quickly. They may threaten trouble or say an offer will disappear.
Scammers pretend to be trusted people or organizations. Caller ID can be faked.
Requests to pay only by gift card, cryptocurrency, or wire transfer are major warning signs.
An unexpected message asking for personal or financial information can be a scam.
Phishing messages try to steal information through emails or texts. They may claim an account has a problem or include an unexpected invoice.
For a suspicious message, contact the organization through a website or number you already know is real.
A company’s logo does not prove a message is real.
If someone already paid a scammer, they should contact their bank or payment provider immediately and ask whether the payment can be reversed. Recovery is not guaranteed.
- Rules it must follow: The AI should never give a fully wrong answer, by saying that a message is completely safe even when the AI knows it can't confirm that.
- How to answer: The AI should start by clearly stating the intention of the text message, and then a further analysis with an explanation or other information and precautions.
